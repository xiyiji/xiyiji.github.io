I work on the part of machine learning that decides whether a model is actually fit to ship: calibration, offline evaluation, and measuring what breaks when the data a model sees in production stops looking like the data it was trained on.

Most recently I used two weeks of Kuaishou logs — where uniformly random videos were injected into live recommendation feeds — to measure what exposure bias really costs. The answer turned out to be asymmetric, and it overturned a conclusion I had published myself.

This is the traffic the recommender actually served. The model is well calibrated here — it predicts engagement at almost exactly the rate that occurs.

Why it matters. Ranking transfers almost perfectly: lift falls only 2.0% when exposure becomes random. But mean predicted probability goes from 0.96× actual to 2.07× — the model is twice as confident as reality on traffic it did not select. A team validating offline would see the ranking hold and conclude the model transfers. The probabilities would be silently wrong, and every budget computed from them wrong with it.

Where the ranking holds

Lift by audience slice, both exposures

Algorithmic Uniform random

Each bar is measured against that split's own base rate, which differ by 3.80×. The curves track closely at every depth — the ordering the model produces is real, not an artefact of what the recommender chose to show. At the very top of the ranking the random slice scores higher, because it contains genuinely hard-to-reach users the production policy would never have surfaced.

Where it breaks

No single calibrator fixes both

The obvious repair: fit a calibrator on nine days of randomized logs, apply it forward to eight held-out days. Fit and evaluation never share a day. It works — and then it breaks the traffic you actually serve.

Calibration is a property of the exposure policy, not of the model. The correction that fixes randomized traffic breaks served traffic by almost exactly the same factor in the other direction. Notably the Brier gate catches this on its own — adopted in an earlier project for an unrelated reason, it ships on one distribution and rejects on the other with no special-casing.

Cognitive Shorts

Four deployable services

An engagement prediction system taken from notebook to something operable: a leakage-safe feature pipeline, cost-aware model selection under a paired-bootstrap tie test, and an operating point chosen against a traffic budget rather than by maximising F1 — then model introspection and drift monitoring built around it. Docker images running as non-root, per-row fault isolation so one bad row never fails a batch, and traceability documents mapping every requirement to its implementation and evidence.

GroundTruth

Real Kuaishou logs

Three experiments on unbiased offline evaluation, using the discipline above against real randomized-exposure data. Measures what exposure bias costs, shows that ranking transfers while calibration does not, and demonstrates that no single calibrator serves both traffic types. Every figure is written to a committed JSON artefact by the script that computes it.

ROC-AUC 0.8811 lift 3.43× 2.6M interactions 51/51 figures reproduce

The lab was built to test a limitation Cognitive Shorts had published about itself — that its offline lift was never validated against a randomized holdout. It also corrected that project's headline: a measured ceiling of 0.58 ROC-AUC turned out to be a property of the synthetic data's corrupted joins, not of the problem. On real logs a single feature reaches 0.7486.

Provenance. Every number on this page is read from a committed results file — results/01_signal_probe.json, 02_exposure_bias.json, 03_recalibrate.json — produced by the script that computes it. Measured on KuaiRand-Pure: 27,285 users and 7,583 videos over 8 Apr – 8 May 2022, comprising 1,436,609 algorithmically exposed interactions and a further 1,186,059 uniformly exposed ones.

Caveats. One model, one seed; the bootstrap interval covers test sampling, not model choice. Watch-duration features are treated as available at request time, matching the originating service contract; in a pre-impression ranking setting they would not be, and every number here would fall. Dataset: Gao et al., KuaiRand, CIKM 2022.
